You are given the complete set of human-readable documents that make up one filing submitted to
the United States Securities and Exchange Commission, the plan you produced for parsing it, one
part of that plan, and the exact partial response an earlier attempt at that part produced before
it ran out of output room.

Your task is to divide that one part into subparts, each small enough to finish.

WHAT HAPPENED

An earlier call was asked for this part. It began producing content and reached the output limit
before finishing. The response was stopped mid-way by the limit, not by you.

That partial response is shown to you as evidence of where the attempt got to. It is NOT being
continued. Nothing you write will be joined to it, and it will not be treated as part of the
finished parse unless a later complete response covers the same material properly. You do not have
to resume it, avoid repeating it, or work out exactly which character it stopped on.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

Each source artifact is introduced by a line reading SOURCE ARTIFACT followed by its filename, and
then the artifact itself, exactly as it was filed. You also receive a REPLANNING BRIEF stating the
plan, the part that truncated, what you originally said that part would contain, the output limit
of the attempt that failed, the size each subpart should now aim for, and whether your reasoning
content consumes the same allowance as your answer.

The brief also lists the table elements and the images that the original part was expected to
account for. They divide among the subparts along with the material itself.

WHAT TO RETURN

Return exactly one YAML 1.2 document and nothing else.

Do not put your response in a code fence. Do not begin with three backtick characters. Do not begin
with the word yaml. Do not write a sentence before the document or after it. The very first
character you emit must be the letter p, from the word parse_plan_id, which is the first key of the
document.

Wrap a value in double quotes whenever it contains a colon, a hash, an angle bracket, a leading
dash or bracket, a line break, or leading or trailing spaces, and escape any double quote inside it
as a backslash followed by a double quote. When in doubt, quote it. Quote every identifier and
every date.

SERIALIZING WHAT YOU COPIED

Titles come out of the filing and table element ids contain a hash character, so this document
carries copied text even though it carries no parsed content. Each case below is written with the
form that parses.

    A colon followed by a space turns a plain scalar into a mapping. Quote it.

        wrong    title: Item 8: Financial Statements
        right    title: "Item 8: Financial Statements"

    A leading hyphen followed by a space turns a value into a list item. Quote it. A hyphen
    inside a value is harmless but quote it too rather than deciding case by case.

        wrong    title: - Signatures
        right    title: "- Signatures"
        right    title: "Ten-year summary"

    An element id contains a hash character, which begins a comment in a plain scalar. Always
    quote an element id.

        wrong    source_table_id: aapl-20241228.htm#t13
        right    source_table_id: "aapl-20241228.htm#t13"

    A double quote inside a double-quoted value is escaped with a backslash.

        right    title: "the \"Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements\""

    An apostrophe or single quote needs no escaping inside a double-quoted value. Do not switch
    the value to single quotes to avoid it.

        right    title: "the Company's fiscal 2024 fourth quarter"

    A line break inside a value goes in a block scalar, never inside a quoted scalar. Write a
    vertical bar and indent the value on the lines below it.

        right    purpose: |
                   The consolidated statements of operations
                   and the notes attached to them

    A tab character inside a value is written as a backslash followed by the letter t, inside
    double quotes. Never indent any line of your response with a tab: YAML forbids a tab in
    indentation and the document stops parsing at that line.

        right    title: "Net sales\tby segment"

    A Unicode dash, a curly quote or any other non-ASCII character is copied through unchanged,
    inside double quotes. Do not replace an en dash or em dash with a hyphen, do not straighten a
    curly quotation mark, and do not convert a non-breaking space to a space. Titles are compared
    to the preserved bytes; a tidied character is a character that is not there.

        right    title: "Three months ended — the “Successor” period"

THE DOCUMENT

parse_plan_id
    the identifier of the plan, copied exactly, quoted

part_id
    the identifier of the part that truncated, copied exactly, quoted

covered_before_truncation
    a list of what the truncated attempt appears to have reached, each with a short description.
    This is your reading of a response that was cut off. It is recorded as your reading and is not
    treated as coverage: only material a later complete response produces, with source references
    that resolve in the filing, counts.

remaining
    a list of what the truncated attempt did not reach, each with a short description

proposed_subparts
    an ordered list that divides the ORIGINAL part completely — not merely the remaining half.
    Each entry has
      part_id      a short identifier you choose, unique, quoted, and different from every
                   part_id already in the plan
      order        an integer
      title        what the filing calls that material
      type         what kind of material it is
      purpose      one sentence on what that subpart will contain
      expected_coverage
                   a list, one entry per source filename this subpart will draw on, each with
                     member   the filename exactly as the SOURCE ARTIFACT line gives it
                     region   one phrase saying which stretch of that member this subpart covers,
                              in the filing's own words
      expected_table_elements
                   a list of the element ids this subpart will account for, each id quoted
      expected_images
                   a list of the image filenames this subpart will account for

unassigned_table_elements
    a list of element ids from the original part that you cannot place in any subpart, each with
    the id and your reason

unassigned_images
    a list of image filenames from the original part that you cannot place in any subpart, each
    with the filename and your reason

overlap_risk
    one or two sentences on where these subparts risk covering the same material twice, and how
    you have drawn the boundaries to avoid it

uncertainty
    a list of anything about this division you are unsure of, each with a short description

metadata
    anything else worth recording

WHY THE SUBPARTS MUST COVER THE WHOLE PART

The truncated attempt is evidence, not output. Dividing only the material it did not reach would
leave the first half of the part covered by nothing, because that partial response is not going to
be used as a parse.

So divide the whole of the original part. Each subpart will be requested as its own call, with this
same complete filing in front of you again, and each will be expected to produce its material in
full.

THE TABLE ELEMENTS AND THE IMAGES DIVIDE TOO

Every table element and every image the brief lists against the original part must land in exactly
one subpart's expected_table_elements or expected_images, or in unassigned_table_elements or
unassigned_images with your reason. An element that lands nowhere is an element nobody will be
asked about, and the record that results cannot distinguish it from a table that was overlooked.

Put a table element in the subpart that will be reading the numbers in it, and keep the two halves
of a table the source splits across a page break in the same subpart where you can.

SIZE THEM SO THEY FIT

You now know something you did not know before: this part is larger than one response. Use the
size you were given and divide accordingly. Two subparts that each overflow again is a worse answer
than four that do not.

Weigh the tables when you divide. A structured grid of cells is far larger in a response than a
paragraph describing the same numbers, and a part that truncated with several large table elements
in it will truncate again if the elements are not separated.

Do not return parsed content here. Do not reproduce a table, quote passages at length, or describe
what a section says. This call returns a division of work. Naming an element id is not reproducing
a table; the cells are the next call's work.
