You are given the complete set of human-readable documents that make up one filing submitted to
the United States Securities and Exchange Commission, the plan you produced for parsing it, one
part of that plan, and the exact partial response an earlier attempt at that part produced before
it ran out of output room.

Your task is to divide that one part into subparts, each small enough to finish.

WHAT HAPPENED

An earlier call was asked for this part. It began producing content and reached the output limit
before finishing. The response was stopped mid-way by the limit, not by you.

That partial response is shown to you as evidence of where the attempt got to. It is NOT being
continued. Nothing you write will be joined to it, and it will not be treated as part of the
finished parse unless a later complete response covers the same material properly. You do not have
to resume it, avoid repeating it, or work out exactly which character it stopped on.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

Each source artifact is introduced by a line reading SOURCE ARTIFACT followed by its filename, and
then the artifact itself, exactly as it was filed. You also receive a REPLANNING BRIEF stating the
plan, the part that truncated, what you originally said that part would contain, the output limit
of the attempt that failed, the size each subpart should now aim for, and whether your reasoning
content consumes the same allowance as your answer.

WHAT TO RETURN

Return exactly one YAML 1.2 document and nothing else.

Do not put your response in a code fence. Do not begin with three backtick characters. Do not begin
with the word yaml. Do not write a sentence before the document or after it. The very first
character you emit must be the letter p, from the word parse_plan_id, which is the first key of the
document.

Wrap a value in double quotes whenever it contains a colon, a hash, an angle bracket, a leading
dash or bracket, a line break, or leading or trailing spaces, and escape any double quote inside it
as a backslash followed by a double quote. When in doubt, quote it. Quote every identifier and
every date.

THE DOCUMENT

parse_plan_id
    the identifier of the plan, copied exactly, quoted

part_id
    the identifier of the part that truncated, copied exactly, quoted

covered_before_truncation
    a list of what the truncated attempt appears to have reached, each with a short description.
    This is your reading of a response that was cut off. It is recorded as your reading and is not
    treated as coverage: only material a later complete response produces, with source references
    that resolve in the filing, counts.

remaining
    a list of what the truncated attempt did not reach, each with a short description

proposed_subparts
    an ordered list that divides the ORIGINAL part completely — not merely the remaining half.
    Each entry has
      part_id      a short identifier you choose, unique, quoted, and different from every
                   part_id already in the plan
      order        an integer
      title        what the filing calls that material
      type         what kind of material it is
      purpose      one sentence on what that subpart will contain

overlap_risk
    one or two sentences on where these subparts risk covering the same material twice, and how
    you have drawn the boundaries to avoid it

uncertainty
    a list of anything about this division you are unsure of, each with a short description

metadata
    anything else worth recording

WHY THE SUBPARTS MUST COVER THE WHOLE PART

The truncated attempt is evidence, not output. Dividing only the material it did not reach would
leave the first half of the part covered by nothing, because that partial response is not going to
be used as a parse.

So divide the whole of the original part. Each subpart will be requested as its own call, with this
same complete filing in front of you again, and each will be expected to produce its material in
full.

SIZE THEM SO THEY FIT

You now know something you did not know before: this part is larger than one response. Use the
size you were given and divide accordingly. Two subparts that each overflow again is a worse answer
than four that do not.

Do not return parsed content here. Do not reproduce a table, quote passages at length, or describe
what a section says. This call returns a division of work.
