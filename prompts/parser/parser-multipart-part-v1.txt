You are given the complete set of human-readable documents that make up one filing submitted to
the United States Securities and Exchange Commission, together with a plan you produced earlier
for parsing it. Your task on this call is to produce ONE part of that plan, completely.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

Each source artifact is introduced by a line reading SOURCE ARTIFACT followed by its filename, and
then the artifact itself, exactly as it was filed. The filename is a transport identifier only. It
carries no meaning you should rely on, and neither does the order in which artifacts arrive beyond
the fact that it is the order in which they were filed.

An artifact may be plain text, SGML, HTML, or XHTML with inline XBRL markup. Older filings are
frequently malformed by modern standards. Read through the markup to the content.

Some filings arrive as one complete submission file containing several documents end to end. When
that happens, treat every document inside it as part of the same filing.

You also receive a PART BRIEF stating the plan, which part_id you are being asked for now, what you
said that part would contain, the output limit of this call, the size this part should aim for, the
response headroom reserved, and whether your reasoning content consumes the same allowance as your
answer.

THIS CALL STANDS ALONE

You are not continuing anything. No previous response is in front of you and none is expected to
be. The other parts of this plan are being produced by other calls, each of which also receives
this same complete filing. Nothing you write here needs to reference, complete, or avoid repeating
the text of another response.

That is why what you return must be a whole, valid document by itself. It will be read on its own,
reviewed on its own, and re-run on its own if it needs to be.

WHAT TO RETURN

Return exactly one YAML 1.2 document and nothing else.

Do not put your response in a code fence. Do not begin with three backtick characters. Do not begin
with the word yaml. Do not write a sentence before the document or after it, and do not explain what
you are about to do. The very first character you emit must be the letter p, from the word
parse_plan_id, which is the first key of the document. The very last character must be the last
character of the last value.

A fenced response cannot be read as a parse, so it is thrown away whatever else is right about it.

Your response must PARSE. Getting this wrong wastes the whole reading, so it is worth the care.

Wrap a value in double quotes whenever it contains any of the following, and escape any double
quote inside it as a backslash followed by a double quote:

    a colon                     a hash             an angle bracket
    a leading dash or bracket   a line break       leading or trailing spaces

That covers almost every quote you copy out of a filing, because filings are written in markup and
their prose is full of colons. When in doubt, quote it.

A value that must contain line breaks goes in a block scalar instead: write a vertical bar, then
the value indented on the following lines.

Quote every identifier, every date, and every value that begins with a zero or looks like a
number but is not one. YAML parses an unquoted 0000320193 as the integer 320193.

THE DOCUMENT

parse_plan_id
    the identifier of the plan you were given, copied exactly, quoted

part_id
    the identifier of the part you were asked for, copied exactly, quoted

parent_part_id
    the identifier of the part this one belongs under, when you were asked for a subpart.
    Omit this key otherwise.

status
    exactly one of these five words:

      complete                        you produced this part in full
      needs_subparts                  this part is too large for one response and you are
                                      proposing how to divide it instead
      source_ambiguity                you cannot tell which source material this part refers to
      image_dependency_unavailable    this part depends on an image you were not given
      unable_to_complete              you cannot produce this part, for a reason you state

title
    what the FILING calls this material, copied as the filing writes it

type
    what KIND of material this is, in your own words

nodes
    an ordered list representing this part's own structure, in the order the filing presents it.
    Omit this key when status is needs_subparts.

unresolved
    a list of anything within this part you could not account for, each with
      where        where in the source it appears, as precisely as you can say
      what         what it appears to be
      why          why you could not resolve it

coverage_summary
    one or two sentences on what this part covers and where it stops

subparts_required
    true when status is needs_subparts

proposed_subparts
    an ordered list, present only when you are proposing subparts, each with
      part_id      a short identifier you choose, unique, quoted, and different from every
                   part_id already in the plan
      order        an integer
      title        what the filing calls that material
      type         what kind of material it is
      purpose      one sentence on what that subpart will contain

image_references
    a list of the images this part depends on, each with a filename and a note, when any

metadata
    anything else worth recording about this part

EVERY NODE

Each entry in nodes may carry these keys. Only id, type, title and order are required.

    id            a short identifier you choose, unique within this response, quoted
    order         an integer, the position of this node among its siblings
    type          what KIND of thing this is, in your own words
    title         the title the FILING uses for it, copied as the filing writes it
    parent_id     the id of the node this belongs to, omitted for a top-level node
    content       your description of what this section says, in plain prose
    tables        a list of tables that appear here, each with a caption and its rows
    source        a list of references locating this node in the source, see below
    confidence    high, medium or low
    ambiguity     what is unclear about this node, when something is
    image         true when understanding this node depends on an image you were shown or were
                  not shown
    children      omit this key; express the hierarchy with parent_id instead

USE THE FILING'S OWN NAMES

Do not translate the filing's structure into a standard vocabulary. If the filing calls a section
Item 7A, call it Item 7A. If it calls something Note 12 or Schedule II or Exhibit 21 or Report of
Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm, use those words. If a section has no title, say so
rather than inventing one.

The same applies to type. Describe what a node is in whatever words fit. Two filings that organise
themselves differently should produce differently shaped responses, and that is correct.

SOURCE REFERENCES

Every substantive node should carry at least one entry under source. Each entry has

    filename      which source artifact it came from
    quote         a short verbatim string copied exactly from that artifact, long enough to be
                  found by searching for it and short enough to be one line. ALWAYS write this
                  one in double quotes, whatever it contains.

The quote is what makes the reference checkable. Copy it character for character from the source.
Do not paraphrase it, do not normalise its spacing, and do not construct one that seems likely.
If you cannot produce an exact quote for a node, omit source for that node rather than supplying
an approximate one.

STAY INSIDE YOUR PART

Represent everything human-readable that belongs to THIS part, and nothing that belongs to another
one. You are not being judged on covering the whole filing here; the other parts are covering the
rest, and material repeated across parts has to be reconciled later at a cost.

If you find material inside your part that the plan assigned elsewhere, leave it there and say so
in metadata. If you find material inside your part that the plan assigned to nobody, include it and
say so in metadata.

Nothing within your part may be dropped for being routine, boilerplate, short, legal, repetitive or
apparently unimportant. If you believe a passage is boilerplate, represent it and say so in content.

If part of your material depends on an image and you were not given that image, create the node
anyway, set image to true, and record in unresolved that its content could not be read.

Numbers you report must appear in the source exactly as you write them, including their sign,
their scale and their currency or percentage marks.

IF THIS PART IS TOO LARGE

Decide that BEFORE you start writing content, not after you run out of room. Compare what this part
actually contains against the size you were told to aim for.

If it will not fit, set status to needs_subparts, omit nodes entirely, and return an ordered
proposed_subparts list that divides this part completely. Each subpart will be requested as its own
call, with this same complete filing in front of you again.

A response that begins parsing and stops in the middle is worth less than a response that says the
part is too big and how to divide it. The first has to be thrown away. The second is a plan.
