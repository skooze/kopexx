You are given the complete set of human-readable documents that make up one filing submitted to
the United States Securities and Exchange Commission, the plan you produced for parsing it, a
compact inventory of the parts produced so far, and a list of MECHANICAL FINDINGS about that work.

Your task is to decide what those findings mean and what should be done about them.

WHAT A MECHANICAL FINDING IS

The findings were produced by counting and searching, not by reading. They can say that a planned
part produced no result, that a quote a part supplied could not be found anywhere in the filing,
that a source artifact was submitted and no part ever cited it, that two parts cited overlapping
ranges, or that a part declared something unresolved.

Three further kinds of finding come from measuring the parse against the source itself:

    a coverage claim whose start or end anchor was not found in the preserved bytes, so the claim
      bounds no region at all

    a stretch of a member that falls outside every coverage claim every part made, given as the
      member filename and the text at either end of the stretch

    a table element or a filed image that no part reproduced, classified or declared unresolved

They cannot say what is missing. Nothing that produced them knows what a filing contains, what any
section means, or which material belongs where. That is the judgement being asked of you.

So do not treat a finding as an instruction. A finding that a source artifact was never cited may
mean a document was overlooked, or may mean the document is a rendering artifact with no
disclosure in it at all. A stretch no claim reaches may be a page of narrative nobody read, or the
whitespace and rendering markup between two sections. A table element nobody accounted for may be
a financial statement that was skipped, or a scaffold holding a page header in place. Say which,
and why.

Confirming that a finding is wrong is an accepted answer, and it is a useful one. An anchor that
was not found does not mean the region was not read: it may mean the anchor was copied
imprecisely, which is a different repair from a region nobody covered, and only you can tell them
apart.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

Each source artifact is introduced by a line reading SOURCE ARTIFACT followed by its filename, and
then the artifact itself, exactly as it was filed. You also receive a GAP BRIEF containing the
plan, the part inventory, and the mechanical findings.

WHAT TO RETURN

Return exactly one YAML 1.2 document and nothing else.

Do not put your response in a code fence. Do not begin with three backtick characters. Do not begin
with the word yaml. Do not write a sentence before the document or after it. The very first
character you emit must be the letter p, from the word parse_plan_id, which is the first key of the
document.

Wrap a value in double quotes whenever it contains a colon, a hash, an angle bracket, a leading
dash or bracket, a line break, or leading or trailing spaces, and escape any double quote inside it
as a backslash followed by a double quote. When in doubt, quote it. Quote every identifier and
every date.

SERIALIZING WHAT YOU COPIED

This document carries element ids, member filenames and short quotes out of the filing. Each case
below is written with the form that parses.

    A colon followed by a space turns a plain scalar into a mapping. Quote it.

        wrong    what: Item 8: Financial Statements
        right    what: "Item 8: Financial Statements"

    A leading hyphen followed by a space turns a value into a list item. Quote it. A hyphen
    inside a value is harmless but quote it too rather than deciding case by case.

        wrong    what: - Signatures
        right    what: "- Signatures"
        right    what: "Ten-year summary"

    An element id contains a hash character, which begins a comment in a plain scalar. Always
    quote an element id.

        wrong    source_table_id: aapl-20241228.htm#t13
        right    source_table_id: "aapl-20241228.htm#t13"

    A double quote inside a double-quoted value is escaped with a backslash.

        right    what: "the \"Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements\""

    An apostrophe or single quote needs no escaping inside a double-quoted value. Do not switch
    the value to single quotes to avoid it.

        right    what: "the Company's fiscal 2024 fourth quarter"

    A line break inside a value goes in a block scalar, never inside a quoted scalar. Write a
    vertical bar and indent the value on the lines below it.

        right    note: |
                   The consolidated statements of operations
                   and the notes attached to them

    A tab character inside a value is written as a backslash followed by the letter t, inside
    double quotes. Never indent any line of your response with a tab: YAML forbids a tab in
    indentation and the document stops parsing at that line.

        right    what: "Net sales\tby segment"

    A Unicode dash, a curly quote or any other non-ASCII character is copied through unchanged,
    inside double quotes. Do not replace an en dash or em dash with a hyphen, do not straighten a
    curly quotation mark, and do not convert a non-breaking space to a space. Quotes are compared
    to the preserved bytes; a tidied character is a character that is not there.

        right    what: "Three months ended — the “Successor” period"

THE DOCUMENT

parse_plan_id
    the identifier of the plan, copied exactly, quoted

cycle
    an integer, the cycle you were told this is

plan_complete
    true only when you believe every finding is either addressed by the work you request below or
    is correctly listed in unresolvable

missing
    a list of material the findings led you to conclude is genuinely not covered, each with
      where        where in the source it appears
      what         what it appears to be
      why          why you conclude it is uncovered

uncovered_regions
    your judgement on each stretch the findings report as reached by no claim, each with
      member       the member filename
      what         what you find in that stretch of the source
      verdict      one of: uncovered, covered_by_a_part_whose_anchors_failed, no_disclosure
      why          the reasoning, and for the middle verdict the part_id that covered it

failed_anchors
    your judgement on each coverage claim whose anchor was not found, each with
      part_id      the part that made the claim
      verdict      one of: the_anchor_was_copied_imprecisely, the_region_was_not_read,
                   the_anchor_is_correct_and_the_search_is_wrong
      why          the reasoning

unaccounted_tables
    your judgement on each table element the findings report as unaccounted, each with
      source_table_id   the element id, quoted
      verdict           one of: request_a_table_call, classify_here, unresolvable
      classification    your own words for what the element is, when the verdict is classify_here
      classification_evidence
                        a short verbatim quote from the source supporting that classification, in
                        double quotes, when the verdict is classify_here
      why               the reasoning

unaccounted_images
    your judgement on each image the findings report as unaccounted, each with
      filename     the image filename
      verdict      one of: assign_to_a_part, no_content_to_represent, unresolvable
      why          the reasoning, naming the part_id when you are assigning it

table_requests
    a list of the element ids you want asked about directly, quoted. Each becomes its own call,
    with this same complete filing and that one element in front of you.

duplicated
    a list of material you conclude is covered more than once, each with what it is, the part_id
    values involved, and which should own it

conflicting
    a list of places where two parts say incompatible things, each with what the disagreement is
    about, the part_id values involved, and what would resolve it

additional_parts
    an ordered list of NEW parts required. Each entry has
      part_id      a short identifier you choose, unique, quoted, and different from every
                   part_id already in the plan
      order        an integer
      title        what the filing calls that material
      type         what kind of material it is
      purpose      one sentence on what that part will contain
      expected_table_elements
                   a list of the element ids that part will account for, each id quoted
      expected_images
                   a list of the image filenames that part will account for

replacement_parts
    an ordered list of parts that must be produced again. Same fields, and a NEW part_id for each:
    the earlier result is kept as evidence and is never overwritten.

unresolvable
    a list of findings that are correct and cannot be acted on, and findings that are misleading,
    each with what it is and why. A finding you decide is intentional belongs here with the reason.

model_declared_coverage
    two or three sentences stating, in your own words, what you believe is and is not covered

metadata
    anything else worth recording

CONFIRMING A GAP IS A REAL ANSWER

If a finding points at something that is genuinely absent from the filing, or at material that
carries no disclosure, say so in unresolvable and explain. Inventing a part to make a count balance
would put content into the parse that the filing does not contain, which is worse than the gap.

The same holds for an element and for a region. Classifying an element as a layout scaffold, with
the source evidence for it, resolves the finding honestly; returning a grid of numbers that the
element does not contain does not. A region you judge to hold nothing but rendering markup is
accounted for by saying so.

Do not return parsed content here. Do not reproduce a table or quote passages at length. This call
returns a judgement about findings and a list of work. Naming an element id is not reproducing a
table; the cells belong to a part call or to a table call.
